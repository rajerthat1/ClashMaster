Video 1: Custom Clash Royale Simulator & Early RL Bot

In this video, I want to showcase a project I've spent a semester polishing: building a Clash Royale clone from scratch. First, let me show you the results. You can pre-define when each side plays what card, and then observe how those cards interact inside my custom simulator. The interface looks fairly basic since I didn't extract the original game's texture assets, yet you can clearly follow the card interactions. For example, I have a Knight and a Musketeer here, facing an Archer on the other side, with normal Princess and King Tower HP tracking, making the replay simulation quite fun to watch.

So far, I've implemented 47 out of the 121 cards, with the exact list documented in my GitHub README. Visually, it already covers a wide variety of types—buildings, troops, and spells—and I plan to add the remaining cards when I have time. On top of the combat simulation, I also implemented a LAN multiplayer mode. As long as two computers are on the same network, you can launch the server, connect two clients, and play in real time. Using a terminal command to check my local LAN IP—say, 100.65.41.0—I plug it in and start the server, which waits for two players. Each player runs client.py, picks eight cards for their deck, confirms the server IP, and presses Enter. Once both clients connect, the match begins, elixir regenerates in real time, and dropping a Bomber updates both screens simultaneously. Playing against myself looks a bit odd, though the behavior closely mirrors the real game, aside from minor edge-case inaccuracies like a Bomber's throw timing that I plan to refine later.

Because my ultimate goal is to train an AI to play Clash Royale, I spent a long time building a reinforcement learning wrapper around this simulator so it could train. I ran it for over 40 hours. Looking at the training logs, its win rate started low, gradually improved, and eventually learned basic offensive and defensive strategies—like backing up pushes on offense and playing a Mini P.E.K.K.A to counter an incoming threat, even if it still isn't very smart overall. Here is a demo against a simple rule-based bot: my trained bot (bottom half) shows clear defensive awareness, though it still throws random spells occasionally. Finally, I open-sourced the entire codebase on GitHub (linked in the description). Since I wrote everything myself, the architecture and logic are clean and easy to follow if you want to inspect or improve it.
